Engineering the corners

Learner guide • Paths A and B • Human decisions, AI assistance and evidence • Session 3 → final project

1. Why there are two paths

We need a working starting car before testing improvements. Some learners can finish a clean lap with the original controller; others cannot. The paths describe your starting situation, not your skill level. Both lead to the same workflow: preserve a working baseline, experiment in a working copy, record laps and test one improvement at a time.

A clean lap means a complete timed lap with zero damage at the start and end. A car merely moving is not yet a clean baseline. Fix installation or connection problems before choosing a driving path.

4. Add the driving recorder to the working copy

You do not type lap readings manually. Bob adds a recorder inside snakeoil3_v2.py. Every time that working copy drives, the recorder appends readings to lap_data.csv. Each row is one driving step, not one lap. A run_id separates different script launches; lap_id separates laps within a run.

Unlike a recorder that overwrites the CSV at startup, this guide preserves the same cumulative file. For example, run R01 contains warm-up plus laps 1 and 2; run R02 contains a later experiment. Those records remain in the table. Never compare rows without checking their run and lap IDs.

Create a logging-only version first and test it. Use it as the measured starting point for corner changes. Recording can affect timing, so compare logged versions with the same logging settings. Keep snakeoil3_v1.py unchanged.

6. Read the data like a driver

Example only: distFromStart 500, speedX 80, accel 0 and brake 0.2 means “500 metres around the track, we are going 80 km/h, easing off the accelerator and applying a light brake command”, assuming standard SCR units. A short forward sensor means the edge is close along that ray; it may reflect a corner, car heading or position. It is not automatically the nearest wall or a crash.

trackPos describes sideways position; angle describes heading relative to the track. min_valid_track is the shortest valid sensor ray, not a precise sideways clearance. Damage increases are stronger evidence of contact than a distance reading alone.

Example A: the car approaches quickly, brakes late, runs wide and gains damage. Hypothesis: slowing earlier in this section will improve clean completion. Example B: the car is stable but brakes repeatedly near its target. Hypothesis: a small braking margin will reduce unnecessary braking. Example C: the car changes steering abruptly through a bend. Hypothesis: limiting sudden steering changes will improve stability.

Choose one based on YOUR data. Do not add all three features together. A long straight can justify extra speed only after corner handling is reliable.

9. Understand the corner sequence

LOOK AHEAD: recognise a possible bend. CHOOSE A SPEED: select a cautious target for that road section. SLOW BEFORE TURN-IN: ease off or brake when the controller logic calls for it. TURN SMOOTHLY: avoid sudden corrections. EXIT: gradually accelerate when the road opens and the car is stable.

A dead-band is a small margin before braking; a target of 80 with a margin of 5 would mean considering braking above 85. This is a teaching example, not a recommended setting. Do not brake and accelerate against each other unless the controller explicitly has a justified policy.

10. Race, compare and decide

Reset to the same conditions. Run the changed working copy and record actual results. Aim for at least three comparable complete laps for the previous logged version and the candidate when practical. Preserve damaged and failed runs; do not hide them. Record median clean-lap time, all damage and completion rate. Comparing three laps is a classroom check, not statistical proof. The instructor’s observed 0.55 s difference is not a universal noise threshold.

KEEP provisionally if the result improves and stays clean across comparable trials. ADJUST if there is a useful idea but instability or uncertainty. UNDO if slower or unsafe. A faster damaged lap does not meet the zero-damage goal.
